Commentary

Because we are only looking at a 5×5 grid here, we can reasonably view the entire state space and notice patterns.

When the players start at positions with odd Manhattan-distance parity, the cars tend to move toward the center and dance around the central squares until the game ends, letting each player get the most points out of their turns. Cars often take a slightly suboptimal path to the center, as in P1 (x0, y0) · P2 (x1, y2) , but the general strategy is still clear.

When the players start at positions with a even Manhattan-distance parity, the behavior is more elaborate. The optimal center dance is not possible with even parity, and Player 2 has the advantage, with an incentive to crash. Player 1 stalls once by walking into a wall while Player 2 chases, which shifts the distance parity to odd. After that, both players move toward the center as expected. Stalling at a wall carries a significant penalty of −0.556, equal in magnitude to the reward for landing on the center square. It also means spending more time in the low scoring squares near the walls. With the number of turns allowed, though, this lets Player 1 share the center square and make up for the loss.
